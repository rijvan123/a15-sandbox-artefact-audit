## Artefact tokens alone identify families in MELD-DS-448 but not clearly in WinMET (H1)

A probe that saw only footprint and probing tokens, with every behaviour token masked, reached 40.2 points (36.5 to 42.9) on MELD-DS-448, against 32.0 points (28.8 to 34.8) for a probe that saw only trace length. The difference ψ~1~ = 8.2 points (4.4 to 11.5) exceeded the 3-point margin (component P = 0.0033; Fig. {{F:probes}}a). On WinMET, the artefact-only probe reached 50.7 points (43.6 to 54.1) against 48.4 points (39.8 to 55.4) for length alone: ψ~1~ = 2.4 points (−6.9 to 9.8; P = 0.6091). Trace length alone therefore separated WinMET families almost as well as the artefact tokens did. Because H1 requires both installations, it was inconclusive (intersection–union P = 0.6091; Holm-adjusted P = 1.0; rejection P = 0.782). The exploratory learners showed the same pattern: ψ~1~ was 7.0 points (3.5 to 10.3) on MELD-DS-448 and 7.3 (−5.6 to 14.6) on WinMET for the 1D-CNN, and 10.5 (6.5 to 14.2) and 13.6 (−4.2 to 23.0) for the n-gram LightGBM (Supplementary Table S4).

## Masking footprint and probing tokens costs no more than masking random tokens (H2)

For the Transformer, masking footprint and probing tokens at test time lowered macro-F1 from 47.7 to 43.9 points on MELD-DS-448, and a random mask of the same size, matched to token frequency and position, also left 43.9 points (Fig. {{F:masking}}a). On WinMET the corresponding values were 62.0, 57.3 and 56.8 points. The excess drop ψ~2~ was −0.03 points (−0.96 to 0.95) on MELD-DS-448 and −0.47 points (−2.17 to 1.35) on WinMET. Both lie inside the ±3-point equivalence margin (two one-sided tests, P = 0.0029; Holm-adjusted P = 0.0087), so H2 was rejected. On MELD-DS-448, the exploratory learners' excess drops ranged from −0.04 to 2.9 points (Fig. {{F:masking}}b). On WinMET the n-gram LightGBM was the exception, with ψ~2~ = 18.4 points and a wide interval (−2.1 to 22.3): masking F tokens alone lowered its WinMET macro-F1 from 70.8 to 40.1 points, whereas the Transformer lost 2.4 points (62.0 to 59.6).

## Models do not transfer between installations (H3)

Transformers trained on MELD-DS-448 scored 47.7 points within the installation and 23.3 points (21.5 to 25.1) on WinMET, a gap ψ~3~ of 24.4 points (20.5 to 27.6). Transformers trained on WinMET scored 62.0 points within and 24.1 points (22.5 to 25.3) on MELD-DS-448, a gap of 38.0 points (22.6 to 42.2; Fig. {{F:transfer}}a). Both gaps exceeded the 3-point margin with P = 0.0001, the smallest value attainable with 9,999 replicates (Holm-adjusted P = 0.0004), so H3 was supported. Every learner showed the gap (Fig. {{F:transfer}}b): 21.4 to 27.3 points from MELD-DS-448 to WinMET and 38.0 to 52.8 points from WinMET to MELD-DS-448.

## Training without footprint tokens recovers little of the gap (H4)

Training the Transformer with F tokens masked changed within-installation macro-F1 by −0.2 points (−1.3 to 0.7) on MELD-DS-448 and −0.9 points (−2.9 to 1.4) on WinMET. Trained on MELD-DS-448 and tested on WinMET, it scored 26.3 points (24.2 to 28.0), against 23.3 for the unmasked model; trained on WinMET and tested on MELD-DS-448, it scored 23.9 points (22.2 to 25.6), against 24.1. The recovered share of the pooled gap was ρ = 0.046 (0.014 to 0.084), below the registered 0.10 (rejection P = 0.0043; Holm-adjusted P = 0.0087), so H4 was rejected (Fig. {{F:transfer}}c, d). The exploratory learners recovered between −0.02 and 0.05 of the gap, and F-masked training cost the n-gram LightGBM 6.5 points within WinMET (−13.2 to −3.7). In an exploratory exact McNemar test on seed-majority predictions, F-masked training changed the Transformer's cross-installation prediction for 549 traces, turning 230 errors into correct attributions and 319 correct attributions into errors (P = 0.00017). Across the 50 family-level F1 differences, the median shift was zero (Hodges–Lehmann estimate 0.0; Wilcoxon signed-rank P = 0.84).

Table {{T:learners}} and Fig. {{F:learners}} compare the four learners. Within each installation, the n-gram LightGBM scored highest (51.4 points on MELD-DS-448 and 70.8 on WinMET) and the Transformer lowest (47.7 and 62.0), although all intervals overlapped. Across installations, every learner fell to between 21.6 and 27.1 points when trained on MELD-DS-448 and to between 18.0 and 24.1 points when trained on WinMET. ## Attributions, referral and identification of the analysis machine

Integrated Gradients (IG) gave exactly zero attribution to masked positions in all six Transformer cells, and the completeness criterion was met with 50 quadrature nodes in every cell (Methodology). The base Transformer gave most of its attribution to behaviour tokens (Fig. {{F:probes}}b): 95.8–96.7% behaviour, 0.5–0.7% footprint and 2.6–3.6% probing in MELD-DS-448, and 88.9–94.4%, 3.7–7.8% and 1.9–3.3% in WinMET. Among the 200 test traces explained per cell, the referral rule flagged a trace when its footprint share exceeded the 95th percentile of validation traces. It flagged 3.5–8.0% of traces in each cell, inside the binomial interval for a 5% budget (2–8%). Flagged traces were misattributed more often than accepted ones only in WinMET: at origin 1, 8 of 14 flagged traces (57.1%) against 15.6% of accepted traces (P = 0.005); at origin 2, 3 of 8 (37.5%) against 20.3% (P = 0.182); and at origin 3, 5 of 8 (62.5%) against 24.5% (P = 0.029; Fig. {{F:temporal}}b). In MELD-DS-448, flagged traces were misattributed no more often than accepted ones (P = 0.62–0.92). A probe on the frozen Transformer embeddings identified which of ten MELD-DS-448 analysis machines produced a trace with balanced accuracies of 11.8%, 10.4% and 14.5%, against 10% chance (permutation P = 0.089, 0.404 and 0.003; Fig. {{F:probes}}c). WinMET was produced on one machine, so the probe was not run there.

## Clustering, masking and drift checks

The registered expectation that the design effect of test clusters would be at most 1.2 was not met. Computed over all shared-family test traces, it was 4.56, 4.63 and 2.56 for the MELD-DS-448 test sets and 229.6 for WinMET, where one cluster of 458 redline traces drives the value; the 0.8 rule removes almost all of that cluster. Among the traces actually analysed, design effects were 1.4–1.6 in MELD-DS-448 and 9.4–9.6 in WinMET (computed post hoc from the frozen assembly without rescoring any model). There, 53 of the 54 gcleaner traces form a single cluster, so the WinMET intervals carry almost no sampling variation for that family. The matched random masks had to borrow 8.5–9.1% of their positions from neighbouring frequency bands in five cells and 14.3% at MELD-DS-448 origin 3, above the 10% bound. Over March–August 2025, the origin-1 Transformer scored 43.5, 42.2, 40.9, 46.8, 39.9 and 42.7 points on MELD-DS-448 traces of its families (Kendall τ = −0.20; one-sided exact P = 0.36; area under time 42.6 points), so the predicted monthly decline was not supported. The origin-1 BiLSTM and 1D-CNN showed no decline either (Kendall τ = −0.20 and −0.33; one-sided exact P = 0.36 and 0.235; Fig. {{F:temporal}}a).

## Sensitivity to the near-duplicate threshold

H3 was supported at thresholds of 0.7 (gaps of 22.3 and 33.0 points) and 0.9 (22.2 and 42.2). At the registered thresholds it was inconclusive, although both gaps remained large: 14.5 points (4.0 to 20.5) and 16.6 points (7.7 to 33.0), with 31–34 WinMET traces per origin. H2 was rejected at 0.9 and inconclusive at 0.7 and at the registered thresholds. H4 was rejected at 0.7 and 0.9 and was not tested at the registered thresholds, because H3 was not supported there. H1 was inconclusive at every threshold (Supplementary Table S5). The number of analysed test traces rose from 451 MELD-DS-448 and 97 WinMET traces at the registered thresholds to 1,074 and 617 at 0.7, 1,802 and 975 at 0.8 and 2,404 and 2,538 at 0.9 (Fig. {{F:threshold}}a). # Discussion

## Principal findings and comparison with existing methods

Models that attribute malware to families from sandbox API-call traces did not transfer between two independent installations of the same sandbox. A Transformer trained on one installation lost 24.4 or 38.0 points of family-balanced macro-F1 on the other installation's traces of the same families, retaining 49% and 39% of its within-installation score, and every learner lost between 21.4 and 52.8 points. The size of the loss is comparable with what Kaya et al. reported for detection, where detectors that score above 90% in sandbox evaluations fall to about 20% on endpoint traces and trace standardisation raised transfer between two sandboxes from 53.4% to 60.7% true-positive rate [@kaya2025]. Our setting is narrower, two installations of one open-source sandbox and the same families, yet the loss is of the same order. The tokens most plausibly tied to the installation did not account for it: masking footprint and probing tokens at test time cost the Transformer no more than masking the same number of random tokens of matched frequency, and training without footprint tokens recovered 4.6% of the gap, with an upper bound of 8.4%. The installation dependence is real, but the explanation the study set out to test does not account for it. Tables 1 and {{T:comp}} place these results beside published approaches, most of which were evaluated within one collection environment.

## Possible explanations for the transfer gap

Several explanations remain open, and this design cannot separate them. The families are populated differently: MELD-DS-448 spans 2020 to 2025 and WinMET has no submission dates, so one family name can cover different variants. The labels come from different sources, and MELD-DS-448 labels agreed with ClarAVy on 54.5% of the confident shared binaries that could be checked. The installations may also differ in ways the lexicon classes as behaviour, such as call ordering, timing or run length; the length-only probe reached 48.4 points within WinMET, echoing the spurious trace-length correlation reported for sandbox detectors [@kaya2025]. WinMET's pipeline anonymised sensitive values, discarded executions suspected of evasion and added hooks [@raducu2025dib], differences that our harmonisation reduced but cannot be shown to have removed. A direct test would replay the same binaries in both installations, so that population and installation are no longer confounded; that requires executing malware, which this study did not do. Concept drift adds a further, slower shift [@ceschin2023; @patidar2026], although the origin-1 model showed no monthly decline over six months.

## Differences between learners

The learners differed in where they found family evidence. The Transformer, BiLSTM and 1D-CNN gave at least 88% of their attribution to behaviour tokens in every cell, and masking footprint and probing tokens cost them about as much as masking random tokens. The n-gram LightGBM lost 30.7 points of WinMET macro-F1 when footprint tokens were masked, and training it without them cost 6.5 points within WinMET. Reliance on environment artefacts is therefore a property of the model as well as of the data, and an audit of one learner does not certify another.

## Learner comparison, robustness and cost

A higher score within an installation did not predict a higher score across installations. Among models trained on WinMET, the order of the learners within the installation was exactly reversed on MELD-DS-448 traces, and the n-gram LightGBM, which scored highest within WinMET, lost 52.8 points across installations and 30.7 points when footprint tokens alone were masked. A lexical model on token n-grams can use installation-specific values directly, and these results suggest that it did so on WinMET; the deep sequence models were less sensitive to footprint masking but transferred no better in absolute terms. Model selection by within-installation validation, the usual practice, would therefore have favoured the learner that depended most on its analysis environment. Because only four learners were compared and their intervals overlap, the reversal is a warning rather than a ranking.

The transfer gap was not produced by the near-duplicate rule or by drift within the test period. It exceeded the margin at every threshold, including the registered thresholds that left only 97 WinMET test traces (Fig. {{F:threshold}}), and the monthly scores of the origin-1 models showed no decline from March to August 2025 (Fig. {{F:temporal}}a). The rule itself mattered for the within-installation estimates: about two thirds of the WinMET test traces had a near-identical trace in training (Table {{T:data}}), so an evaluation without near-duplicate control would have scored WinMET models largely on traces they had effectively seen. The referral rule helped only where the model leaned on installation-specific evidence: referred WinMET traces were misattributed more often than accepted ones at two of three origins, whereas referred MELD-DS-448 traces were not (one-sided P ≥ 0.618; Fig. {{F:temporal}}b). Referral is therefore a triage aid, not a substitute for validating the model in the installation that produced the evidence.

The audit is affordable. It ran on a personal computer without a graphics processor: assembly took 1.25 h and the whole evaluation 57.1 h of wall-clock time, and the n-gram LightGBM needed 3.2 job-hours against 158.2 for the Transformer and 214.4 for the BiLSTM (Table {{T:cost}}). A forensic laboratory can therefore repeat the validation for each new installation without special hardware, and the cost of re-validation is not a reason to skip it.

## Implications for forensic practice

For forensic use, the quantity that matters is the error rate on traces from the installation that produced the evidence. The amended Federal Rule of Evidence 702 asks whether an opinion reflects a reliable application of a method to the facts of the case, and the certificate for electronic records under Section 63(4) of India's Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam 2023 records the hash value of the evidence; ISO/IEC 27041 asks that a method's validation cover the conditions of its use [@iso27041]. The cross-installation macro-F1 of 18 to 27 points measured here shows how far an error rate measured on one installation can be from the rate on another, so a validation report that omits the installation is incomplete. The framework addresses this in two ways. Its environment-match gate attaches the error rate validated under matching conditions and refers other traces to an analyst, and its ledger makes the order of freeze, registration and scoring verifiable. The referral rule behaved as designed, flagging close to its 5% budget everywhere, and identified traces at higher risk of misattribution in WinMET (57.1% against 15.6% at origin 1) but not in MELD-DS-448. The footprint share of attribution is therefore a usable triage signal only where footprint tokens carry weight.

# Conclusions, limitations and future work

## Conclusions

This study proposed a framework for collecting behavioural malware evidence in a forensically sound way and for auditing deep-learning family attribution across sandbox installations, and applied it in a pre-registered audit of two independent CAPEv2 installations. Within each installation, the Transformer reached 47.7 and 62.0 points of family-balanced macro-F1; on the other installation’s traces of the same families it reached 23.3 and 24.1 points, and every learner lost between 21.4 and 52.8 points. Masking footprint and probing tokens at test time cost no more than masking random tokens, and training without footprint tokens recovered 4.6% of the gap (95% CI 1.4 to 8.4%). The ledger made the order of freeze, registration and scoring verifiable. For practice, the results imply that a family-attribution model is evidence only for the installation on which it was validated: its error rate must be re-measured before it is applied to traces from another installation, and footprint-free training does not provide portability.

## Limitations

The study has limitations. The primary analysis uses a near-duplicate threshold of 0.8 instead of the registered antimode, because the registered rule left 31–34 WinMET test traces per origin; the deviation was time-stamped before scoring, all analyses come from the same models, and H3 held at 0.7, 0.8 and 0.9 with large estimates at the registered thresholds, whereas H2 and H4 depended on having enough test traces. The test sets are small and clustered, one WinMET family is essentially a single cluster, the registered design-effect bound was not met, and the projected interval half-widths already exceeded the 3-point margin, which explains the inconclusive H1. Label quality was checked for only part of each installation, seven families remained unverified, and two installations of one sandbox cannot show how far the findings extend to other sandboxes or to endpoint telemetry. Traces were cut to 256 tokens, the BiLSTM was trained under a reduced plan, and the lexicon's division into behaviour, footprint and probing tokens was a frozen judgement; tokens it classes as behaviour may still encode the environment. The matched random mask exceeded its borrowing bound in one cell, and adversarial adaptation by malware authors [@ling2023; @macas2024] was outside the scope of the study.

## Future work

Future work should replay identical binaries across installations, extend the audit to more sandboxes, to endpoint data and to learners used in practice, and test whether non-lexical features such as call order, timing and run length explain the transfer gap.
